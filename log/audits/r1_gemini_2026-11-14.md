**Audit Findings (Most Serious First)**

* **[Must-Fix] Contradiction in the central satirical premise: "status page failed to load" vs. "Every light is green"**
* **Where:** Opening shot ("Microsoft's own status page failing to load, captioned 'real screenshot'") vs. Chorus ("All systems operational / Every light is green / Believe the screen") and Fairness to Microsoft (Check #2).
* **What's wrong:** Your opening proves that Microsoft's status page *failed to load*, and your intro/bridge quote actual status updates acknowledging the outage ("We're investigating reports...", "We're seeing renewed errors"). Yet the Chorus attacks the classic status-page trope where a dashboard stubbornly shows all green checkmarks during an outage. If Microsoft's status page actually crashed—or once loaded, admitted the outage in corporate-speak—satirizing them for claiming "All systems operational / Every light is green" is factually unfair to how Microsoft communicated during this specific incident.
* **Proposed fix:** Decide what the narrator actually is. If the real status page crashed or showed a stale cached "green" state before failing, show that explicitly in the opening screenshot so "Every light is green" is earned. Otherwise, make the narrator a fictional *cached* status widget or corporate PR reassurance engine, or adjust the chorus so the joke is about minimizing a massive collapse into polite euphemisms ("Service degradation," "Some users may experience delays") rather than literal green lights.


* **[Must-Fix] Treehouse meeting room contradicts your own IP/likeness exclusion**
* **Where:** Video Plan ("a treehouse meeting room for the bridge, captioned 'dramatization'") vs. Excluded on purpose ("anything resembling... Microsoft's campus treehouses") & Disclosure Card ("the treehouse meeting room in the bridge is drawn from those years").
* **What's wrong:** You state that you are excluding anything resembling Microsoft's campus treehouses, yet you set the Bridge in a treehouse meeting room where Microsoft's incident report is written on a whiteboard, and you explicitly tell the viewer in the Disclosure Card that this room is based on the creator's time at Microsoft. Microsoft's Redmond campus treehouses (built by Pete Nelson in 2017) are iconic, heavily publicized spaces. Having the post-mortem written inside a treehouse directly links the setting to Microsoft's real campus facilities, violating your own rule and muddying whether the scene depicts actual Microsoft engineers.
* **Proposed fix:** Either drop the treehouse setting for the Bridge (use a generic glass-walled conference room or a backstage green room at "Session 503") or stop claiming in your plan that it doesn't resemble Microsoft's treehouses. If you keep the treehouse, do *not* put the Microsoft incident report whiteboard inside it—keep the whiteboard in the fictional radio station's control room so you aren't depicting a dramatized Microsoft war room.


* **[Must-Fix] Real screenshots in the opening likely contain Microsoft trademarks and UI trade dress**

* **What's wrong:** Real screenshots of Outlook and the Microsoft 365 status page almost certainly display the Microsoft logo, the Outlook icon, product wordmarks, or proprietary UI layout, contradicting your claim that "no logo is shown."

* **[Should-Fix] Bridge line "(Not related to AI or agents)" sounds like suspicious corporate denial rather than a verified fact**
* **Where:** Bridge lyrics: `Contributing factors / (Not related to AI or agents)` and Fact Card: `"not related to cyber or AI / agents."`
* **What's wrong:** Because the song's narrator is an unreliable, gaslighting status page ("Nothing here is broken / Everything is fine"), putting `(Not related to AI or agents)` in the narrator's mouth—especially in parentheses right next to `(We're committed to doing better)`—primes the audience to hear it as a classic "the lady doth protest too much" cover-up. Someone who knows nothing about the outage (Check #1 & #7) will walk away thinking AI/Copilot *did* cause the outage and Microsoft is lying about it. Meanwhile, from the opposite angle (Check #3), if taken literally without the "cyber" context, it reads like an inside-baseball exoneration of AI inserted by an AI lyricist.

* **[Should-Fix] Musical imitation risk on the whistled hook + "Hey! / Ho!"**
* **Where:** Intro/Outro (`[whistled hook over acoustic guitar, then stomp-clap]`) + Verse/Chorus (`(Hey!)` / `(Ho!)`).
* **What's wrong:** While stomp-clap-hey is an uncopyrightable genre style (The Lumineers, Of Monsters and Men, Edward Sharpe & The Magnetic Zeros, Mumford & Sons), combining a solo acoustic guitar + whistled intro hook + alternating "Hey!" / "Ho!" gang shouts comes very close to mixing Edward Sharpe's "Home" (whistled intro) with The Lumineers' "Ho Hey." AI music generators trained on those exact reference tracks frequently spit out near-identical melodic intervals when prompted with "whistled hook + stomp-clap + Hey/Ho."
* **Proposed fix:** Replace the whistled hook with a cheerful glockenspiel/xylophone, mandolin, or accordion riff—instruments heavily used in 2010s tech-commercial folk that won't trigger melody-matching or sound like an Edward Sharpe rip-off—and audit the AI-generated audio melody against "Ho Hey" and "Home" before locking the track.
