# All Systems Operational

**AI-generated satire.** A mid-2010s stomp-clap singalong about the Microsoft 365 outage of November 12–13, 2026, filmed as a made-up Seattle public-radio live session, "Session 503". The narrator is the status page, singing in the earnest, everybody-together voice of a 2010s tech ad. Every time the gang vocals swell, the stream goes down: the band cuts out mid-word, the picture freezes under a buffering spinner, and one handclap keeps going, late. That's the retry collapse: Microsoft's restored servers kept going unresponsive once traffic returned.

> **Status, November 17, 2026: final render.** The final-look render (M7) is under way; the video isn't published yet. It doesn't wait for Microsoft's final post-incident report (due November 20): the creator read the preliminary one (EX1550543/MO1550821, in the Microsoft 365 admin center) and expects the final one to add little.

**Conflict of interest:** the lyrics were drafted by Claude, made by Anthropic. Anthropic's Microsoft 365 connector broke during this outage, and Claude had outages of its own this year. The creator worked on Outlook and Word at Microsoft between 2014 and 2017, and the treehouse meeting room in the bridge is inspired by those years; it doesn't depict how this incident was handled. The disclosure card at the end says all of this. Before production, Gemini (Google) audited the lyrics and the concept (November 14, no web access; `log/audits/`); Claude proposed the responses as the conflicted party and the human decided each one (the brief's audit log). The other audits the brief planned (Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT, Grok, an open-weights model, and a second pass on the final bridge and fact card) weren't run.

**Nothing on screen ties AI-written code to this outage.** Microsoft said on November 13 that the outage was "not related to cyber or AI / agents"; its preliminary report (November 15) traces it to a latent defect from a July fix and doesn't say who or what wrote that fix. The video says exactly that and no more, unless the full report does.

This repository is the production record the video's end card points to. Claude Code produced the video. A human approved each milestone, picked the takes, made the Suno generation and the Logic Pro master, supplied the credits music and made the screenshots. Each of those 37 interventions so far is logged.

**The production log and decisions are written by the agent and are a record, not a statement of fact about Microsoft or anyone else:** where they discuss the outage, the fact card and its sources are the reference.

## What's real, and what's made up

Real, in the opening bookend (0:00–0:07) only:
- **The creator's screenshot (0:02–0:04):** Microsoft's status page failing to load ("real screenshot · Thu, Nov 12, 1:07 p.m. ET"), opening close on the tab and the address bar. The tab's loading indicator, frozen in the capture, is drawn turning over it; nothing else in it is changed. It's the only real logo and interface in the video, there so the made-up status page never reads as Microsoft's. A second capture, a "No license found" error, was a placeholder and is cut from the video; one small tile of it remains in an early contact sheet in the history (`review/m3_screens.jpg`).
- **The creator's post (0:04–0:07):** the creator sharing this song in their #it-self-help channel, recreated in a generic chat UI and captioned "recreation". The creator's own name and photo are the one real-person exception, with their approval.

Made up, everywhere else:
- **The outage-report spike (0:00–0:02):** an unlabeled chart drawn from reported Downdetector counts ([GV Wire, Nov 12](https://gvwire.com/2026/11/12/microsoft-outlook-down-for-thousands-of-users-downdetector-shows/)), not a capture.
- **The status page, the station and the session:** "Session 503" at 50.3 FM (an impossible frequency) is a fictional broadcast, not affiliated with KEXP or any real radio station. It evokes Seattle's live-session format without any station's name, logo or design.
- **The #it-help chat** (invented names) and **the treehouse meeting room** are captioned "dramatization". The treehouse doesn't depict where or how the real incident was handled.
- **The whiteboard** sings the bridge back: root cause, contributing factors, lessons learned. On "(Not related to cyber, AI or agents)" two tags land: "Microsoft, Nov 13, two days before its root-cause report." and "The report doesn't say who or what wrote the July fix."

## Sources

### The fact card, verbatim

Four pages after the song. Microsoft's preliminary report carries a no-reproduction notice, so the card quotes a few words of it at most and never shows its pages.

1. Exchange Online problems began at 11:20 a.m. ET on Thursday, Nov 12, per Microsoft's preliminary report, and the wider outage followed at 1:26 p.m. *([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/microsoft-365-outage-resolved/))*. It hit Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, Copilot and Defender in at least 74 countries *([Wire](https://wire.com/en/blog/microsoft-365-outage-november-2026))*.
2. Almost 130,000 Outlook problem reports came from U.S. users just after 1 p.m. *([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/13/microsoft-races-address-outage-affecting-users-around-world/))*.
3. Some businesses fell back on fax machines to confirm trades *([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/microsoft-365-outage-74-countries/))*.
4. Restored servers kept going unresponsive once traffic returned *([Azure Blog UK](https://azureblog.co.uk/posts/microsoft-365-outage-12-november-2026/))*.
5. Anthropic and OpenAI both reported their Microsoft 365 connectors failing *([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/microsoft-365-massive-outage-fixes-fail-as-restored-servers-keep-going-unresponsive/))*.
6. Microsoft's preliminary post-incident report (Nov 15; EX1550543/MO1550821 in the Microsoft 365 admin center) names three factors: a latent defect from a July fix to its networking gateway servers, which its load tests missed because they "tended to focus on weaker hardware"; a routine Teams failover onto the same gateway; and nearly 14% of capacity out from an unrelated issue. Automatic rerouting spread the failure until more than 80% of servers were unresponsive *([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/saas/2026/11/16/microsoft-blames-monster-email-outage-on-software-defect-introduced-in-july-fix/5310026))*. Impact ended Friday, Nov 13, at 11:10 a.m. ET. Final report due Nov 20.
7. On Nov 13 Microsoft said the outage was "not related to cyber or AI / agents" *([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/13/microsoft-365-outage-resolving/))*. That came two days before its preliminary report, which doesn't mention AI or say who or what wrote the July fix.
8. **Format:** "Session 503" is a fictional broadcast and is not affiliated with KEXP or any real radio station.

The brief lists every source the production used ([`docs/brief.md`](docs/brief.md#sources)). The links are as of the brief's revision 141 (November 16); they're reopened before the upload.

## Reproducing it

Large files (audio, screenshots, the figure library, generated stills and clips, renders, the edit) are not in git; [`MANIFEST.sha256`](MANIFEST.sha256) lists them with checksums. The production archive is the [v1.0 release](https://github.com/Derivative-Work/all-systems-operational/releases), built by [`scripts/release.sh`](scripts/release.sh). It holds:

- the two upload files (the main video and the Short), with the captions and the thumbnail;
- `inputs.tar`: the audio that git doesn't hold and that can't be regenerated;
- `takes.tar`: every generated still and clip, used or not;
- `SHA256SUMS`.

Everything else regenerates. The commands are in the production log, step by step, and each script's header says what it reads and writes.
- **Environment:** `uv sync` (Python, pinned in `uv.lock`), `PUPPETEER_SKIP_DOWNLOAD=1 npm install` (Remotion 4.0.534), Blender 5.2, and ComfyUI for the generated shots.
- **Seeds:** every random process reads its seed from [`config/seeds.json`](config/seeds.json) (main seed 41). Stills and clips with the same seed, weights and ComfyUI version should match on the same GPU (production used an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090, 32 GB); expect small differences elsewhere.
- **Can't be regenerated:** the song (Suno doesn't give the same song twice), the Logic master, and the credits music. The post-filter model is private for now, so the archive keeps the post-filtered audio.
- **Not in the archive:**
  - the creator's captures: the Slack screenshots, supplied as text-style reference only (private), and the real screenshot and photo in the opening bookend;
  - Apple's Logic loop in the clap track, used under Apple's terms;
  - the figure library, rebuilt from [`blender/ep/`](blender/ep/);
  - the Claude Code session transcript, which holds the private captures.
